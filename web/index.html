Twin plate vector displacement

Two PNGs are the model.

A solid can be stored as a pair of images. Both plates sit on the same center. One faces up, the other faces down, and each pixel is the vector from that center out to the surface. A mesh of any size is a resample of those two files.

plate + plate − resampled mesh

Shape, from a formula

1 · Formula

The solid is math: a sphere, a box, a torus, a cone, a capsule, a mushroom, a rippled ball. Nothing is loaded from a modeling tool.

2 · Outward rays

Both plates occupy the same center. The upper face fires up. The lower face fires down. Lean splays both rays away from the middle, so the stored vector picks up X and Z.

3 · Two files

Hit minus plate point is the vector. Red, green, and blue store X, Y, and Z in 16-bit PNG. Alpha is zero where the ray misses. That pair is the model.

4 · Any grid

Reconstruction decodes the PNGs, adds the vectors back onto the plates, and stitches a wall around the silhouette. A coarser grid is a lighter mesh from the same files.

P(u,v) = O(u,v) + (channel / 65535 − ½) × 5

The two PNGs are the geometry source. A build for a console or a phone can resample them at that machine’s vertex budget, then paint albedo and normals in an external tool. Texture size stays independent of this mesh. These files are standard 16-bit RGBA PNGs with stored pixels, so other apps can open them. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.
